Named for a queen who took it with her afternoon tea, the Victoria sponge is the plainest cake in the repertoire and the hardest to hide behind. There is no frosting to cover a sunken middle. Get the creaming right and it is faultless.

## Ingredients

**For the sponge**

- 225g unsalted butter, properly softened
- 225g caster sugar
- 4 large eggs, at room temperature
- 225g self-raising flour
- 1 tsp baking powder
- 2 tbsp whole milk
- 1 tsp vanilla extract

**To fill**

- 150g raspberry jam
- 250ml double cream, whipped to soft peaks
- Icing sugar, for dusting

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan). Butter two 20cm sandwich tins and line the bases with parchment.
2. Beat the butter and sugar together for a full **five minutes**, until the mixture is pale and noticeably increased in volume. This is where the lift comes from — do not rush it.
3. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well between each. If the mixture looks like it might split, add a tablespoon of the flour.
4. Sift in the flour and baking powder and fold through with a large metal spoon, cutting rather than stirring. Loosen with the milk and vanilla to a reluctant dropping consistency.
5. Divide between the tins, level the tops, and bake for 22–25 minutes until golden and just shrinking from the sides.
6. Cool in the tins for 5 minutes, then turn out onto a rack and cool completely before filling.

## Assembly

Spread jam over the bottom layer, right to the edge. Spoon the cream over the jam and set the second sponge on top. Dust with icing sugar just before serving — do it earlier and it dissolves.

## Tips

- **Room temperature is not optional.** Cold eggs will curdle the batter and cold butter will not aerate.
- Weigh your eggs in their shells; whatever they come to is the weight you want of butter, sugar and flour. That is the original recipe.
- Fill it on the day you serve it. A sponge is at its best within hours.
