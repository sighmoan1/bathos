Traditionally eaten on Bonfire Night, parkin is the rare cake that is genuinely bad on the day you bake it and excellent a week later. The oatmeal absorbs moisture from the treacle over time, and the whole thing turns dark and dense and faintly sticky on top.

## Ingredients

- 200g golden syrup
- 100g black treacle
- 175g unsalted butter
- 125g dark muscovado sugar
- 175g plain flour
- 100g medium oatmeal
- 3 tsp ground ginger
- 1 tsp ground cinnamon
- ½ tsp freshly grated nutmeg
- 2 tsp bicarbonate of soda
- ½ tsp fine salt
- 2 large eggs, beaten
- 100ml whole milk
- 50g stem ginger, finely chopped (optional but recommended)

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 160°C (140°C fan). Line a 20cm square tin, base and sides.
2. Warm the syrup, treacle, butter and muscovado sugar in a pan over low heat until the butter has melted and the sugar dissolved. **Do not let it boil** — boiling makes the finished cake hard. Set aside to cool for ten minutes.
3. Combine the flour, oatmeal, spices, bicarbonate and salt in a large bowl.
4. Pour in the warm syrup mixture and stir, then add the eggs and milk and beat to a smooth, loose batter. Stir in the stem ginger.
5. Pour into the tin and bake for 50–60 minutes, until firm at the edges with a slight give in the middle.
6. Cool completely in the tin.

## The waiting

Wrap the whole slab, still in its parchment, in foil and put it in an airtight tin. Leave it for **at least three days**, ideally five to seven. It will darken, soften and become sticky on the surface. Cut it into squares only when you are ready to eat it.

## Tips

- Medium oatmeal, not porridge oats and not oat flour. The texture is the character of the cake.
- Weigh the syrup and treacle by setting the pan directly on the scales — far easier than measuring spoons.
- It keeps for a fortnight, and there is no day within that fortnight when it is not worth eating.
