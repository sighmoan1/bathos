Every failure mode of a baked cheesecake — cracking, sinking, a grainy texture — comes down to the same two causes: too much air beaten in, and too rapid a change in temperature. Solve those and this is not a difficult cake, only a slow one.

## Ingredients

**For the base**

- 200g digestive biscuits
- 90g unsalted butter, melted
- 1 tbsp caster sugar

**For the filling**

- 900g full-fat cream cheese, at room temperature
- 250g caster sugar
- 3 tbsp plain flour
- 4 large eggs, plus 1 yolk
- 150ml soured cream
- 2 tsp vanilla extract
- Zest of 1 lemon
- Pinch of salt

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 160°C (140°C fan). Line the base of a 23cm springform tin and wrap the outside tightly in two layers of foil — this keeps the water bath out.
2. Blitz the biscuits to crumbs, stir through the melted butter and sugar, and press firmly into the base. Bake 10 minutes, then cool.
3. Beat the cream cheese on **low** speed until smooth. Low speed throughout — every bubble you beat in becomes a crack later.
4. Add the sugar and flour and beat until just combined, scraping the bowl thoroughly.
5. Add the eggs and yolk one at a time on the lowest setting, then the soured cream, vanilla, lemon zest and salt.
6. Pour over the base. Set the tin in a roasting dish and pour boiling water halfway up the sides.
7. Bake for 60–70 minutes. The edges should be set and the centre should still wobble like a loose jelly when nudged.
8. Turn the oven off, wedge the door open with a wooden spoon, and leave the cheesecake inside for one hour. Then refrigerate for at least six hours, ideally overnight.

## Tips

- If it cracks anyway, it cooled too fast. It will taste identical; cover it with soured cream or fruit.
- Run a thin knife around the edge as soon as it comes out of the oven, so the cake can contract without tearing itself.
- Cut with a hot, dry knife, wiping between every slice.
