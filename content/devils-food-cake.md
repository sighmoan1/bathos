Angel food cake is white, airy and virtuous. This is the answer to it: darker, heavier and considerably better. The coffee does not make it taste of coffee — it makes the chocolate taste more like chocolate.

## Ingredients

**For the cake**

- 175g plain flour
- 75g good cocoa powder, Dutch-processed
- 1½ tsp bicarbonate of soda
- ½ tsp fine salt
- 300g light brown soft sugar
- 175ml buttermilk
- 175ml hot strong coffee
- 125ml flavourless oil
- 2 large eggs
- 1 tsp vanilla extract

**For the frosting**

- 200g dark chocolate (70%), chopped
- 200ml double cream
- 50g unsalted butter, cubed
- Pinch of flaky salt

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 170°C (150°C fan). Line two 20cm tins.
2. Whisk the flour, cocoa, bicarbonate and salt together in a large bowl. Break up any lumps of cocoa with your fingers.
3. In a second bowl whisk the sugar, buttermilk, oil, eggs and vanilla until smooth.
4. Pour the wet into the dry and whisk to combine, then pour in the **hot** coffee. The batter will look alarmingly thin. This is correct — trust it.
5. Divide between the tins and bake 30–35 minutes, until a skewer comes out with a few damp crumbs. Do not bake until clean.
6. Cool in the tins for 10 minutes, then turn out. This cake is fragile while warm.

## The frosting

Heat the cream until it just begins to steam, then pour it over the chopped chocolate and leave for two minutes without stirring. Stir from the centre outwards until glossy, then beat in the butter a cube at a time. Leave at room temperature until thick enough to hold a swirl, about an hour.

## Tips

- Dutch-processed cocoa is worth seeking out — natural cocoa is more acidic and will make the crumb redder and drier.
- If the frosting splits, add a tablespoon of warm cream and beat hard. It will come back.
- Better on day two. The bicarbonate needs time to settle and the crumb goes properly fudgy.
