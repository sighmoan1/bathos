The failure of most coffee cakes is timidity — a teaspoon of instant granules and a beige result. Use espresso powder, use more than feels sensible, and let the bitterness stand up to the sugar.

## Ingredients

**For the sponge**

- 225g unsalted butter, softened
- 225g light brown soft sugar
- 4 large eggs
- 225g self-raising flour
- 1 tsp baking powder
- 100g walnuts, toasted and finely chopped
- 2 tbsp espresso powder dissolved in 2 tbsp boiling water, cooled

**For the buttercream**

- 250g unsalted butter, softened
- 400g icing sugar, sifted
- 1½ tbsp espresso powder dissolved in 1 tbsp boiling water, cooled
- Pinch of salt
- 12 walnut halves, to decorate

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan). Line two 20cm tins.
2. Toast the walnuts for 8 minutes, cool, then chop — reserve the halves for the top.
3. Cream the butter and brown sugar until pale and fluffy, then beat in the eggs one at a time.
4. Fold in the flour and baking powder, followed by the chopped walnuts and the cooled coffee.
5. Divide between the tins and bake 25–30 minutes, until springy to the touch.
6. Cool completely before filling.

## The buttercream

Beat the butter alone for three minutes until almost white — this is what stops buttercream tasting greasy. Add the icing sugar in two additions, then the coffee and salt, and beat for another two minutes.

## Assembly

Sandwich the sponges with a third of the buttercream, spread the rest over the top, and mark it into rough swirls. Arrange the walnut halves around the edge, one per slice, so everyone knows where to cut.

## Tips

- Espresso powder is not instant coffee. It dissolves properly and is far stronger — worth buying a jar.
- Always cool the coffee before adding it. Warm liquid will melt the butter out of the batter.
